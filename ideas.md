# Visualiser Ideas

The overall direction: minimal full-screen WebGL fields with subtle audio response—more like paintings that listen than conventional music visualisers.

## 1. Rothko Breathing Chapel

A full-screen abstract painting with two or three large colour fields. The music does not animate it obviously; it makes the colours breathe, the edges blur, and the inner light pulse.

- **Best for:** Ambient, classical, jazz, meditation, deep work
- **Audio mapping:** Bass expands the colour blocks; mids shift warmth; treble adds grain shimmer
- **Mood:** Sacred, slow, expensive silence

## 2. Living Ink Wash

The screen becomes a Japanese or Chinese ink painting that never fully forms. Black, grey, and faint coloured washes drift across a pale background. Notes create soft blooms, like ink touching wet paper.

- **Best for:** Piano, ambient, voice, cello
- **Audio mapping:** Bass creates large ink blooms; treble creates fine feathering; silence lets the ink dissolve
- **Mood:** Wabi-sabi, contemplative, monastic tea with a GPU

## 3. Aurora Veil

Very minimal northern-light curtains move slowly across the screen. No sharp particles or rave lasers—just translucent veils of colour folding through darkness.

- **Best for:** Electronic, ambient, cinematic, choral
- **Audio mapping:** Bass controls vertical wave depth; mids control colour drift; treble creates faint sparkling edges
- **Mood:** Celestial, night sky, dream weather

## 4. Desert Light Field

A warm screen of sand, ochre, rust, and pale sky tones. The music creates heat shimmer, slow gradients, and distant horizon movement. Almost nothing happens, but somehow the room changes.

- **Best for:** Folk, acoustic, oud, ambient, slow electronic
- **Audio mapping:** Bass shifts the horizon; mids move warm gradients; treble creates mirage shimmer
- **Mood:** Tarkovsky in a desert monastery

## 5. Ocean Floor

Not waves on the surface, but slow underwater light caustics, dark blues, drifting particulate matter, and soft pressure. The music becomes movement through deep water.

- **Best for:** Downtempo, dub, ambient, deep bass, sleep music
- **Audio mapping:** Bass controls pressure pulses; mids move currents; treble creates floating plankton
- **Mood:** Submarine cathedral

## 6. Plant Intelligence

The whole screen becomes a restrained mycelial or root network. Lines grow slowly, retract, reconnect, and glow faintly. It should feel botanical, not techy.

- **Best for:** Organic electronic, field recordings, experimental music
- **Audio mapping:** Bass thickens root paths; mids create branching; treble creates tiny glowing nodes
- **Mood:** Forest nervous system

## 7. Stained Glass Without Glass

Large abstract panes of muted colour slowly shift, as if light is passing through old glass. The edges are imperfect. The music changes the internal glow rather than the geometry.

- **Best for:** Sacred music, ambient, classical, choral, jazz
- **Audio mapping:** Bass brightens deeper panes; mids alter colour temperature; treble adds tiny imperfections
- **Mood:** Chapel window after midnight

## 8. Cosmic Dust Painting

A black or very dark canvas with slow nebula-like clouds, avoiding cliché space wallpaper. Keep it abstract and sparse; the stars should barely exist.

- **Best for:** Ambient, synth, orchestral, deep electronic
- **Audio mapping:** Bass expands cloud mass; mids move colour fog; treble reveals tiny dust points
- **Mood:** Universe before it decided to become loud

## 9. Minimal Paper Pulse

An almost blank screen in cream, off-white, and faint grey. The music creates subtle paper fibres, pressure marks, and barely visible pulses. This is the anti-visualiser.

- **Best for:** Deep work, meditation, quiet rooms, galleries
- **Audio mapping:** Bass creates slow paper breathing; mids add warmth; treble creates fibre detail
- **Mood:** Silence with a pulse

## 10. Fire Behind Silk

A dark fabric-like surface with hidden ember light beneath it. Music causes the hidden light to swell and fade. No flames—just the suggestion of fire.

- **Best for:** Voice, cello, slow electronic, ritual music
- **Audio mapping:** Bass controls ember glow; mids move fabric folds; treble creates tiny sparks under the surface
- **Mood:** Campfire seen through closed eyes

## 11. Calligraphy Field

Abstract calligraphic strokes appear and fade, but never become readable letters. The music writes gestures across the screen. This could connect beautifully to Eternal Forest and Evgenia’s visual language.

- **Best for:** Poetry readings, voice, experimental music, slow jazz
- **Audio mapping:** Bass controls stroke thickness; mids guide movement; treble creates dry-brush texture
- **Mood:** Language before language

## 12. Slow Geometry Mandala

A barely visible geometric structure rotates and breathes. Think sacred geometry dissolved into mist, not a psychedelic screensaver from 2003. The form should appear only when the music asks politely.

- **Best for:** Drone, ambient, Indian classical, electronic, meditation
- **Audio mapping:** Bass controls scale; mids control rotation; treble reveals line detail
- **Mood:** Temple blueprint made of fog

## 13. Weather System

The whole screen becomes a private atmosphere: clouds, pressure, haze, rain traces, and soft fronts moving across the field. Music shapes the weather.

- **Best for:** Generative music, piano, soundscapes, ambient
- **Audio mapping:** Bass creates cloud density; mids move wind; treble creates rain or mist
- **Mood:** Emotional meteorology

## 14. Zen Equalizer Stones

Instead of bars, use large abstract stone forms. The forms shift weight, balance, and shadow with the music—an equalizer disguised as a rock garden.

- **Best for:** Everything quiet to mid-tempo
- **Audio mapping:** Bass shifts large stones; mids adjust shadows; treble adds gravel texture
- **Mood:** A Japanese garden quietly listening

## 15. Archive of Light

A dark background with slow rectangular traces, like old slides, memory fragments, or fading photographs. The music reveals and erases them, creating a memory-palace feeling.

- **Best for:** Nostalgic music, field recordings, spoken word, cinematic scores
- **Audio mapping:** Bass brings large memory panels forward; mids shift opacity; treble adds film grain
- **Mood:** Dreams stored in a museum drawer

## Strongest First Three

1. **Rothko Breathing Chapel** — Already started and has the clearest identity.
2. **Living Ink Wash** — Stays minimal but feels more alive; it could look beautiful as a Mac screensaver.
3. **Plant Intelligence** — Gives the project a unique signature and could become more than a visualiser: a living symbolic system.

## Guiding Principle

The danger is making it too “music visualiser.” The better path is: **make paintings that listen.**
